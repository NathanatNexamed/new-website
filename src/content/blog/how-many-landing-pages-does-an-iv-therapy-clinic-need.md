A clinic with IV hydration, NAD+ therapy and vitamin injections does not automatically need three different websites. But if you're paying to promote all three services, sending every click to the homepage usually makes the visitor do unnecessary work.

The real question is **whether people searching for those services have different questions and different reasons to book**.

Someone who searches for IV hydration might want to know how long the appointment takes or what options are offered. Someone researching NAD+ may need a more detailed explanation, pricing context and a chance to talk to the clinic. Those are different conversations.

## Start with the services you actually want to grow

We look at three things before recommending a landing page:

1. **Search intent.** Are people looking for a specific service, or are they browsing general wellness options?
2. **Commercial priority.** Does the practice have the staffing, margin and capacity to take more appointments for this treatment?
3. **Booking friction.** Can a visitor understand the service, trust the provider and take the next step without hunting around the site?

If only IV hydration is being advertised, build that page well before duplicating the entire website for other treatments. If NAD+ and injections have meaningful search demand and separate campaigns, dedicated pages may make sense.

## What a strong treatment landing page explains

The page should make the next decision easier. Depending on the treatment, that means:

- The specific service in the headline, matching the ad or search.
- Who provides it and what happens at an appointment.
- Typical visit length and transparent price information where possible.
- Clinically reviewed eligibility, limitations or safety information.
- Real practice photos, location and relevant credentials.
- One clear action: book the appointment, request a consult or ask a question.

Avoid copying the same generic "unlock your best self" headline across every service. A prospective patient wants to know **what happens if they choose your clinic**, not how many adjectives your agency can fit above the fold.

## What about Google Ads Quality Score?

Landing page experience and relevance can influence ad quality, but that doesn't mean you should manufacture dozens of nearly identical pages just to chase a metric. The bigger objective is consistency between what someone searched, what the ad promised and what the page actually explains.

We also look at phone calls and consult requests separately. A click on a phone number is not automatically a completed appointment, and a page with a high form-conversion rate may still produce poor-quality inquiries.

## When one page is enough

If two offerings are essentially variations of the same service, share the same booking process and answer the same questions, one strong page with clear sections may be more useful than two thin pages.

Build pages around **distinct patient intent**, not arbitrary keyword permutations. Then use real lead quality and booking feedback to determine whether the added pages helped.
