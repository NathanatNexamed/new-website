If a clinic's Google Ads report shows a 110% conversion rate, that does not mean 110 patients booked for every 100 visitors. It usually means the account is counting more than one action for some ad interactions.

We have reviewed practice accounts where page views, calls, button clicks and forms were all treated as primary outcomes. It made the dashboard look busy, but it couldn't answer the owner's real question: **Which ads produced genuine inquiries, and what happened to them?**

## Why the number gets inflated

Google Ads can record different conversion actions from the same ad interaction. Imagine someone who:

1. Opens the treatment page.
2. Clicks the phone number.
3. Submits a consultation form.

If each action is counted in the reporting column, one visitor can be associated with multiple conversions. Depending on attribution, counting settings and campaign reporting, the overall conversion count can exceed the number of ad interactions used in the rate calculation.

That's a measurement issue, not proof of extraordinary campaign performance.

## Primary and secondary conversions do different jobs

Primary conversion actions generally inform bidding for campaigns using those goals. Secondary actions can still help diagnose the journey without automatically steering that bidding.

A page view might be useful to observe, but it should not carry the same weight as a qualified phone inquiry. The right configuration depends on the campaign goal and how the clinic handles different lead types.

We typically start by checking:

- What each conversion action actually fires on.
- Whether the same action fires multiple times.
- Whether a call click is being confused with a connected phone call.
- Which goals are included in campaign bidding.
- Whether form submissions are real, valid inquiries.
- Whether the CRM can show which inquiries eventually booked.

## Fix the measurement before changing every campaign

When the conversion setup is wrong, it becomes hard to trust recommendations based on cost per conversion. We would rather report a lower but meaningful conversion rate than a spectacular number built from incidental actions.

For [Pure Hydration](/case-studies/pure-hydration/), the recorded case study describes an account where too many events were being counted as primary. After the measurement and campaign structure were rebuilt, the reported rate for **calls and form fills** was 12–15% over the documented six-month period.

That is a call-and-form conversion metric. It is **not** a claim that 12–15% of ad clicks became paying patients.

## Can we optimize toward booked appointments instead?

Sometimes. If the practice can reliably match a permitted booking event with an ad click, offline conversion imports can be valuable. But healthcare privacy requirements, consent, platform restrictions and the available EMR or CRM fields all matter.

An OptiMantra workflow may expose a different set of fields than Cerbo or Zenoti. We verify the data path instead of promising an identical integration for every clinic.

The real improvement is not a fancier dashboard. It's being able to say, with appropriate confidence, **which inquiries were useful and what the front desk did with them**.
