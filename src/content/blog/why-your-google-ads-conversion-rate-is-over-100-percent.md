We audit a lot of practice Google Ads accounts. One of the most common things we find is a conversion rate that can't be real: 80%, 110%, sometimes higher.

It's almost never because the ads are that good. It's because the account is counting things that aren't patients.

## How it happens

Google Ads lets you create as many conversion actions as you want: calls, form submissions, page views, button clicks, chat starts, GA4 imports. Each one can be set to **primary** or **secondary**.

Primary conversions do two things. They show up in the Conversions column, and Smart Bidding optimizes toward them. When everything is primary, a single visitor who views the pricing page, clicks the phone number and submits a form counts as three conversions.

So the reported numbers inflate, and the bidding learns to find people who click around, not people who book.

## What to do instead

1. **Pick one or two primary actions** that a real patient takes: a qualified call (60+ seconds) and a submitted consult request.
2. **Demote everything else to secondary.** You can still see them; they just stop steering the bidding.
3. **Count one conversion per click** for lead actions, not every.
4. **Import offline conversions** from your CRM for booked and closed patients, once the basics are clean.

## What changes when you fix it

Your reported conversion rate will drop. That's the point. The number you're left with is one you can make decisions on, and the bidding starts learning from people who actually call.

When we did this for [Pure Hydration](/case-studies/pure-hydration/), the account settled at a 12–15% conversion rate on calls and form fills and held there for six months.
